READING IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. THE CONTROL IS CLEAN. Every near-fixed cell is exactly zero and
     3229 of 6561 cells are empty. AA and BB
     are indistinguishable outside the inversion, which is what
     exchangeability requires. This is the strongest control pass in
     the project and it is what licenses reading the body at all.
  2. NO EXACT FIXED DIFFERENCES, in either region -- and that is
     EXPECTED, not a failure. dxy/pi_I = 1.385 means between-class
     divergence is only ~1.4x within-BB diversity, i.e. the classes are
     nowhere near reciprocal monophyly. Complete sorting across all 160
     chromosomes should be rare, and it is.
  3. NEAR-FIXED DIFFERENTIATION IS REAL AND LARGE: 2,219 sites in the
     body against ZERO in the control. This is the answer the called
     VCF could not give -- its version of this test failed its own
     control (sec 8.23).
  4. THE BARRIER SIGNATURE IS CLEAN: shared polymorphism collapses
     from 47.2% to 21.4% of variable sites while private
     variation rises in both classes. That is arrangements sorting
     behind a recombination barrier, and it is symmetric in AA/BB so
     it is the least reference-sensitive statistic here.
  5. THE DIRECTIONAL ASYMMETRY IS NOT INTERPRETABLE AS BIOLOGY.
     Near-fixed runs 7.3:1 toward AA-ALT/BB-REF. The reference IS a
     BB haplotype inside the inversion, so BB-derived variants shared
     with the reference are invisible by construction. The control
     cannot remove this -- outside the inversion the reference carries
     no arrangement identity. Treat the counts as lower bounds.
  6. AA IS NOT BEING LOST TO MAPPING BIAS: AA-private exceeds
     BB-private in the body (41.4% vs 37.2%). Severe reference-mapping
     loss would deplete AA, and it does not.

  LIMITATION: a 2D SFS carries COUNTS, not POSITIONS. It cannot say
  which genes the near-fixed sites are in. That needs per-site output
  (`realSFS fst print`) intersected with degenotate, which is the
  natural follow-on.
